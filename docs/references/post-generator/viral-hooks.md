# Hooks: situation first

Toad's face never changes, so the hook is the situation. Start from one everyday moment the reader is living right now (monday, one more meeting, sunday night, the weekend, "one quick thing"). Then let the image show Toad calmly inside it. Steal the shape here, never the words.

## What batch 001 approved (posted)

1. `no perfect days required. / the pond saved you a seat 🐸 / $TOAD`
   Image: Toad waving from a pond-dock stool, empty stool beside him.
   Why it worked: a site line plus a warm invite, and the image **literalizes the line** (the empty stool is the seat). The reader is the guest. No price, no lore.
2. `"one more episode" / — me, four episodes ago / sunday is a tomorrow problem 🐸 $TOAD`
   Image: Toad on a sofa at night with popcorn and a blanket, late-night clock on the wall.
   Why it worked: a quote-and-time-skip everyone has lived, then a brand phrase ("tomorrow problem") as the punchline. The poster speaks as a pond member ("me"), not as the project.

Shared traits: a real everyday moment, lowercase, 2–3 short lines, one 🐸, $TOAD once, a scene that only works with that caption. Don't turn the `🐸 $TOAD` ending into a template: at most half a batch ends that way.

## Patterns

**1. Line → prop.** Take a site line and build the prop that makes it literal.
- Good: `no perfect days required. / the pond saved you a seat 🐸` (empty stool beside Toad)
- Bad: `no perfect days required. community is everything 🐸🚀` (abstract, no prop, slogan)

**2. Quote, then time-skip.** A famous small lie, then the receipt.
- Good: `"one more episode" / — me, four episodes ago`
- Bad: `toad loves binge watching on weekends lol` (describes, no turn)

**3. Tiny promise vs reality.** "just one quick thing", "5 more minutes", "i'll reply later".
- Good: `opened the laptop for one quick thing. / it's 6pm. / this feels like a tomorrow problem 🐸` (calendar full of blocks)
- Bad: `productivity is hard but toad keeps going 💪` (motivational poster)

**4. Tomorrow-problem handoff.** Name a chore, hand it to tomorrow.
- Good: `laundry chair is at full capacity. / that's a tomorrow problem 🐸` (Toad on the chair under the pile)
- Bad: `procrastination gang where you at 🐸 $TOAD` (empty call-out)

**5. Calendar collision.** A real day/time the reader is in right now.
- Good: `sunday 23:58. / alarm set for 6. / currently doing his best` (Toad in bed, phone glow)
- Bad: `happy sunday pond!! 🐸🐸` (greeting, no situation)

**6. Status label.** Mono-label deadpan, like the brand board ticker.
- Good: `status: still here. / plan: none. / snacks: yes.` (Toad in a blanket fort)
- Bad: `status: bullish. plan: send it.` (pump talk, banned)

**7. Small honest count.** A life number, never a market number.
- Good: `third coffee. / it's 10:14. / monday again?`
- Bad: `$TOAD up 40% today, imagine next week` (price talk, banned)

**8. Warm invite (use sparingly, max 1 in 4 posts).**
- Good: `rough week? / the pond has room for you 🐸`
- Bad: `join the toad army before it's too late` (FOMO, promise)

## Weak vs strong (from batch 001 drafts that were not picked)

- Weaker: `saturday plan: absolutely nothing. / toad started at 9am and is already ahead of schedule ☕🐸 / same toad. different days.` (three beats, emoji stack, tagline bolted on, narrates Toad in third person)
- Stronger: `saturday plan: nothing. / ahead of schedule ☕` (one turn, the beanbag image carries the rest)

## Rules for every hook

- Situation first, Toad second, $TOAD last (or not at all).
- First line names a concrete moment or object. Never "gm", "who's still here", or lore.
- The caption must need the image and the image must need the caption.
- Write as a pond member ("me", "us"), never as the project or an announcement.
- `same toad. different days.` is a sign-off, not a filler line. Use it at most once every few posts.
- One 🐸 max. No hashtags. No CA. No price, pump, or promise words.
